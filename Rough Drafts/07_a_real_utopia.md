# Part 7: A Real Utopia

Black rain fell from the impossibly dark night. It was difficult to see
even three feet ahead without an umbrella to extend out a few feet. The
campus was absent of life late at night, except for the occasional
drunken group of revelers. The rain made any life impossible to discern.
The few moments when I was not blinded by rain, my eyes would deceive
me. It was a simple trip across campus, or so I thought. I took the
scenic path since the rain has always been a comfort to my mind. But
tonight was something different — perhaps the biting wind that gnawed at
your ear, or possibly the chill that forced its way down my spine.

The ominous brick buildings loomed distantly in my path, providing a
safe haven that would bring me back home. I felt the heavy rain fall on
my hair and distort my vision. The safest way to return was through the
school building. A brief flash of lightning and a break in the wind
aligned perfectly for me to see what was ahead — or what was not ahead.
The sudden flash showed a desolate wasteland that reflected only the
souls of the damned. And just as the rain returned, so did reality. I
stood paralyzed in the downpour for a moment before moving on. And
suddenly I felt I was not alone.

The campus hall doors were rightfully locked. The entrance I remembered
was a sunken recess behind a hill. The now pitch-dark, devoid-of-light
hallway forced me to reference the wall to move forward. The sounds of
my shoes resonated off the sparse turns. I felt something wet on the
wall. Turning on my phone, the light glowed on the white surface. I
tried my flashlight, but the battery was too low, leaving me to the
light of my screen.

A dark red and green mixture protruded in lines on the wall; I traced
each letter with my phone. The first word: S T A Y. The second: H I D D
E N. The ring of a flashlight erupted from the side of the hall. "Hey —
mind helping me with that light?" Silence. "Would you mind?" This time I
went toward the flashlight; the light slowly began to shake, then more,
then faster. As I approached the figure, the light dropped perpendicular
to the ground. A gust of wind shivered the hair on my right arm. With
the flashlight still on the ground, it lit the direction the breeze had
blown. A rapid turnaround revealed nothing — nothing but a smell. A
smell that sank into my being. A smell that told me to go back outside
into the rain. A smell that could not follow me outdoors. Still, I
persisted, against better judgment, telling myself that I was real.

I am real, I assured myself, and I will get sick if I go out into the
bitter cold and rain. The path ahead was certain to be safe. Security on
campus was tight; cameras lined every hall. But at this moment, I knew
nobody was watching. The lack of life in this building caused me no harm
— in fact, it was quite relieving. The only trouble I would have was
with the stairs. The stairs that led to my salvation. But on these
stairs lay a note — a note addressed to me. I did not want to read it,
so I did not. It went directly into my left pocket. The stairs going up
into the main areas were much less intimidating than the entrance.

The flashlight flickered, telling me to stop. This way is dangerous.
BANG. A door shut from behind, locking me between the two halls. Two
options now: go up — the faster way to my dorm — or go down, adding
length to the trip. Since both doors were open, I went down. The
likelihood of survival increased with unpredictability. Each breath I
drew pulled the crisp cool air into my lungs as it extinguished my
determination to make it out of this place. The stench worsened upon
descent. Then an unnatural wind came from the stairwell above and shoved
me down the stairs, making me drop the flashlight. An abyss appeared
before me. I scurried to pick it up in the darkness. To no avail. The
flashlight was gone — and so was my light. The mark that struck my soul
was that of oppression. A violent screech came from behind.

It was like a bird — a bird that had died in my hands as a child. But
this bird was long dead, and so was the sound that brought terror to my
heart. Looking back up the stairwell, cloaked in darkness, I saw a
vaguely discernible being. It was roughly humanoid, but the grasshopper
claws and horse hooves stopped me from believing it human. The flaming
white eyes showed me what it had seen and told me all that I needed to
know. I slammed and barricaded the door with a nearby chair and ran into
a nearby laboratory. It was not entirely impossible that it would leave
with impatience or at daylight. I thought about this for quite some time
— but neither the door nor the light held it back.

I turned on the gas in the lab and hid in a closet, preparing my lucky
lighter for if it entered. The smell of propane dulled the odor of this
creature and masked it like a perfume. The shadow entered the room and
scuttled right in front of my closet. I could tell only by the heavy
thuds that shook my lungs with each rapid step. Then they stopped — and
in that silence, I heard its breath. Or the lack of breath; for what I
heard were cries. The presence of this being brought the already dark
closet to dim even further.

As day broke through the cracks in the closet, I heard distant howling
screams. The small rectangular fire-escape window showed me all I had to
see. The figure had left the building and now roamed freely in what
should have been daylight. Or was it daylight? I could tell the sun was
in the sky — but a treacherous night seeped from the ground, removing
the sun's use and causing great desaturation. The only light originated
from the beast: a beaming circular glow reflecting in all directions,
like the headlights of a Peterbilt — dooming whichever thing came into
it. This left me confused as to why I had not yet been killed. For I was
inside its glow.

I could hear others dying as well. The darkness spread from the beast
and seeped into nearby houses. The lights slithered with it, slowly
crawling from its head down its claws, blinding all those that saw it. I
stayed in that classroom, peering out occasionally through the
surface-level window. It knew I was in that building — of course it did.
It had known the entire time. It wanted me to watch. It needed me to
watch. It needed me to see its power before coming to me. Now it dug its
claws into the ground, forcing its legs to carry the now kilometer-long
eyes. The earth moved with it as the claws turned to roots and dragged
each corpse it created to the center of campus, where they lay. It
gnawed my fellow professors, ate my peers, chewed the staff, tore apart
the security, and spat out the police. All the while it grew, and the
light illumined circularly from its eyes.

The beast returned at night, cowering from the moon. It had feasted over
the day on the corpses. Its hooves cleaned the floors with the blood of
its victims. It came to me multiple times in my science lab. The light
would seep through the corners of my closet but cause me no harm. The
claws erupting from the ground would carry gifts of rotten meat. It had
taken a liking to my existence. It wanted me to learn. It wanted me to
return home.

The second lesson was to take power into my own control. The great claw
that crushed stone and demolished anything in its path brought me from
my closet. It threw me from the ground and lifted me into the air like a
feather. The rough, crimson leathery hide — wet with blood — wrapped
around me and engulfed everything except my head. I did not fight it;
there was no use. Whatever it wanted, it was not to kill me — it had had
that chance long ago. It instead slowly framed me, changed me, and
helped me adapt. Now my own skin was leather; my hands no longer hands,
my feet no longer feet. Still, I stayed put.

I started to hope for self-survival again, hoping I could disarm this
conformity. But instead, I gave in — leaving my body to its own devices.
I left the classroom to find a victim. The beast did not like this. The
same leather hide broke from the wall and launched me back into my
closet, barricading it so thoroughly that the wood turned to skin, and
skin turned to my skin, and my skin was its skin. It did not speak. But
it did speak. It spoke in my mind; every loose atom in my body was
filled with its presence. "It is not your time." I tried to speak — to
no avail. I was it, and it was me. I was being taught that it was not
good to disobey. My arms were no longer arms; my legs no longer legs.

I had rarely been without pain before this. I had been diagnosed with
OND years ago, the disease slowly strangling my nerves until I was going
blind, with a dull migraine always prominent in my thoughts. Now, in the
darkness, I saw a new light. A clear vision of a grand future — one
where man did not rule and buildings were crumbled. One where comfort
did not matter and no life but ours existed. A perfect world. My body
was no longer my body, my mind no longer my mind, my eyes no longer my
eyes — and they were forced to shut.

I remembered my life, but my memory was flooded with ancient knowledge —
an accumulation of all that was bad, all that was good, all that was,
and all that was not, flooding my mind. I saw the past lives of those
whom I had consumed. I saw the others, encapsulated like myself,
deformed and reshaped into perfection. I saw the pride, envy, wrath,
sloth, greed, gluttony, and lust of humankind. I saw nations rise and
fall; ideas come to prominence and be returned by their antithesis. I
felt the torment of the millennia of the universe. I understood it all.
And it knew I understood. For it transferred a single communicated
thought — a scripture:

I took these words and burned them into my soul, wrote them in my brain,
and praised their very origin. And I was released back to my campus. The
sun had left; the beast with it. My life was given a new, pure purpose.
My vision had changed — become pure, found perfection. For even in the
depths of night, in the shadows of the indoors, what I saw was light,
and light was what I saw. I slunk throughout the hallways, masking
myself in the shadows, and waited for my command.

As I blinked, I was visited by the heathens and heretics my forefather
had killed. As soon as they appeared, they cried. The hallways turned to
blank grassy plains; a dusty brown looming in the air obscured my
vision, but my nose smelled no dust. Their eyes burned with hatred at my
existence. A man appeared at the forefront — though oblivious to the
weeping souls around him, he paused for just a single moment. In this
moment his eyes pierced me, seeming to weep the word: "Devil."

And again I was gone — but an image lay in my mind, one that forced
itself down my throat and implanted its being in my hollow heart. The
consumption that led my desire fell on deaf ears and was lost to blind
eyes. I hated the night and cursed the day. I angrily slammed a door,
found myself transported to the mailroom, with an envelope, pen, and
paper. I bent the world to my will and wrote:

This letter I sent to the great steps of an eternal campus — for I knew
that whoever found it would eventually give in to curiosity.

My soul began to combust along with the hollow that once claimed my
stomach. The hateful deeds that humanity had created in the name of free
will formed neurotically in my body. I had to leave the shadows; I had
to seek the light. My leathery hide swelled into ambiguity; I half
clopped and half levitated to the nearest exit. But instead, I saw the
man from before, entering inside. I hid myself — I knew I could not face
that look again. He blindly walked by, unaware of my presence. In this
moment he seemed familiar, as if I had met him before. A spark of denial
and hatred forced its way into my corroded soul. Deep beneath, still a
spark of humanity heard the man's gaze. I dissipated and searched for a
way to warn this being of good as quickly as possible. I watched his
path — one hand marking the wall, the other stretched out in front. The
path ahead was open hall; the wall blank with opportunity.

I spread further. An innocent bystander locking classroom doors was
inside the building. I sublimated — nobody could hear his cry for help;
he had none. My claws brutally reduced his being to liquid, which I used
to warn this man of my evil. The crimson blood soared with my will to
land in his path — but he could not see it. Noticing a flashlight born
from the innocent, I brought this to assist. He was concerned. But I
could not let him look into my sunken eyes. He would cry out to my
apparition, but it was a hopeless desire; as he approached, his very
being corrupted me with hatred. I had to leave — I had to flee, or else
the human would lose his worth to me.

He would follow me though, forcing my retreat. I filled with rage as he
pursued me — until I was fed up. I tried to tell him to go away, to stop
following me, but nothing came out. Nothing except a screech — I noticed
then that my mouth no longer existed. This made him run toward me, into
the night. For although my being was behind, I was ahead. He dared to
move against me. I felt his grubby life rub against my perfection. I
moved back to my body, contempt permeating the air with a menacing
stench. I knew his exact whereabouts; he would be killed in a moment. I
hurried to the closet where he sat inside — and stood there for a
moment. I observed this human. He had already atoned for his sins and
was looking to make the world a better place. A real utopia.

I left him to aid in this — but in doing so my hatred grew. My
omniscience drove me to madness; murder upon murder racked my mind. The
evil of humanity corrupted my soul, and I had no choice but to judge
which were good and which were bad. But it was not my intent to leave
this planet devoid of life; I had to leave one being. The best way was
to leave but one in my image. I wanted him to watch; I needed him to
watch. And I knew he was watching me. With each corpse I consumed came a
higher purpose — one to envelope the entire world. I thought of the note
in what used to be my pocket. My hide now concealed this, forcing me to
cut deeply into myself and work it loose. I tore the letter open and
read its contents. The hope for a better world and the self-loathing
only affirmed my course. In the wake of this, I brought my human and
turned him into me.
