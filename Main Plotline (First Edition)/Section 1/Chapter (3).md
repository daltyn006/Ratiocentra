Grant flailed, restricted in his stuporous half-waking, his limbs s to answer him. The silk sheets had wound themselves around his ankles sometime in the night and held there, fluttering awkwardly as he threw himself off the mattress, and for one long half-second his own bed became the thing keeping him down. Some gray echo of a strap, the white dome suffocating him, a needle he couldn't see but knew was there. Then his shoulder met the floor and the room reassembled itself into a room, ordinary and cold. He would have lain there for a moment if not for the strident alarm slithering into his ears and prodding at his brain. The sheet was not hard to remove once he regained his wits, only made at all difficult from the now-forming blue bruise forming on his left shoulder making a black target on his otherwise tan skin. The sun shining between the leaves shone jade, lime, and basil across his room illuminating a lustrous forest green within the otherwise standard naturally toned room. Grant headed for the deep crimson night reds which spread around the insides of the home. 

The trip back to his education was made in no rush, it was never required. After the adequate tests were complete nobody was required to do any task, but with many connections made within it became a default for many to keep and occupied orderly day. Hasseokea resided in the city around where it claimed a singular mall to accommodate the centralization of education. The buildings he had become so content with was left behind each year, the people remained. It was odd for those in other years to travel to different buildings as no common purpose existed, still Beorme and Manada had their reasons to move betwixt. Grant had admired how Beorme was able to make so many impossible "medical" connections, the chemicals he required were made completely legal for those who had passed the first testing period, but for those before were much harder to get their hands upon. Beorme was always able to find connections and find things he should not, which brought him to meet Grant and Daneder at a bar. Manada had been a year older than Grant, there weren't that many fish in the sea and Daneder had been too promiscuous for her. Her photographic skills were nothing to be laughed at, although some would say the artistry is more explicit than necessary- which is partially why she had made her way outside of Danader's Den. She had wanted to photograph raw, uncensored emotions.  

The mall was chilled to a point where clothes were almost required- computers hid within the walls and dissipated heat from the exterior walls, simultaneously freezing and warming the interior. While Grant had been provided the list of educational sections within the mall, a subtly inconspicuous room towards the back held his interests, while ignored by most due to the smaller stature it held a enough space for around five to six people comfortably. It happened to be hidden furthest from the entrance, only found due to the singular window giving view of the city of Hasseoka, black asphalt surrounded the elevated talus of the parking lot watching below the colossus clear automatic doors opening like a moat to prepare the people of the future and protect them from anything outside. Christoph noticed the room on his travels on the first day, a monumental find. While every room had a projector which educated any student willing to listen, it's voice inexplicitly ending at the doorway to their rooms, the older stature of the room allowed Grant to enter the room through a simple curl command given as a password. It became a frequent meeting spot to view archived media they found scattered about the servers in the basement. Videos that went back thousands of years, making it very difficult to refine good from bad. At the request of Daneder they would watch long form videos of two guys on a podcast. Anderson enjoyed anything that could make him laugh or improve his comedy, Cristoph found entertainment in short form educational videos- often picking up quickly what contents it held when anyone allowed him control of the computer. Beorme loved to watch psychedelic videos or meaningless things. Grant, on the other hand, preferred a refined taste of adult animation and stop motion. Now, they had a new member and had no clue what to watch.

She seemed entirely oblivious, or perhaps stubbornly immovable, blocking the doorway and forcing Grant to act. Behind her, a neon fluorescent light spilled across the back of the room, threatening to disrupt the quiet semi-formal focus of the screening. Unwilling to shatter the silence during such a serious viewing, Grant reached out, gently cupping her arm to nudge her aside. It required little effort. She stood a full foot shorter than him and weighed next to nothing. Unexpectedly, she wavered under his touch, swaying like a flower in a sudden draft, before swaying back into balance toward the center of the room. Her gaze shifted instantly, dropping the screen to fixate once more on Grant, sweeping him with an all-too-familiar down-and-up evaluation. Once the door was secured, Grant retreated to the back row, taking a seat beside the computer terminal to monitor for electrical hiccups. Manada followed without hesitation. She sat down, opting for a distance far past comfortable—settling directly onto his right thigh and obstructing his view. The two whispered quietly unbeknownst to the rest of the viewers as the whispers only immersed them in the atmosphere of the ship.

 "You breathe like you're waiting for someone to hit you," Manada whispered, her voice low enough that it barely reached the projector a few feet above them. Her soft and cunning voice, devoid of any performative edge—just raw, unnerving precision. Grant kept his eyes locked on the monitor's diagnostic readout, attempting to anchor himself to the flickering numbers. "Well, you're sitting on my leg in a room full of people, Manada. And you're in my line of sight." "They're watching the screen," she replied simply. She didn't adjust her position on his thigh; if anything, she leaned back slightly, her shoulder settling against his chest like a deliberate anchor. "And I'd bet you only hide behind technical duties when you're overwhelmed. You've refreshed the voltage log four times since the projector even flickered." Grants left hand hovered over the keyboard. The cynical, sarcastic deflection he usually kept ready against her bluntness suddenly felt entirely useless. He glanced over at her small profile, illuminated by the cold blue wash of the statistics screen. 
"It's a heavy load on the system," he muttered in a lie, the quiet confession slipping out before his usual filters could catch it. "If the power drops during this reel, I'd have to reset the session. And everyone in this room would turn on me." Manada didn't offer a reassuring smile or a hollow cliché. Instead, she reached up, her small fingers catching his cheek to turn his eyes to hers. "Let them turn," she whispered, her dark eyes holding his with an intensity that had nothing to do with the physical space she was invading. "I don't care about the movie, Grant. I'm sitting here so that when the machine doesn't fail, you have at least one person who knows what it took out of you to keep it running." Grant exhaled a long, silent breath he hadn't realized he'd been holding. His constant, nagging sensation of being watched—of being scrutinized by every shadow in the room—seemed to shrink, any memories of his nightmares turned to dreams, squeezed out by the sheer, unyielding weight of her presence. He didn't try to shift her off his leg. For the first time all evening, he stopped looking at the monitor.

That evening the group reassembled the way it always did, without anyone quite deciding to call it—Anderson shaking the room with his drunken dancing, already singing loud to the softly playing background music before he'd finished sitting down; Beorme orbiting the room in a meandering circle, frantically touching furniture and others like he was checking an inventory only he could read. It almost felt identical to a hundred nights before it. Grant kept circling back to the same paranoia anyway, half-joking, half not: that he could tell if the eyes were watching him even here, even now, that he'd woken up certain someone had been standing at the foot of his bed. "You always say that when you're wasted," Christoph slurred, not quite looking up. "Since when do you say that so much."

Grant didn't have a comfortable answer that wasn't the dream, so he didn't give one. Manada kept trying to walk the conversation somewhere else—away from Grant's seeming paranoia and toward something with more weight to it, the numbers she'd journaled from the assistants who processed the livestock, how easily people said soulless now like it settled something. "We should just leave," she said, not for the first time, and not really to anyone but him. "Go somewhere they haven't finished converting yet." Anderson laughed like it was a bit. Grant didn't. "The entire world is converted, you know this. The world war against crime and evil. Anywhere that hasn't been adapted is too dangerous to live." "I know," she purred, moving in closer, "I'd just like to be away from the rush." Grant took the hint.

Daneder caught Grant by the arm on the way out, ripping him away from Manada, who turned around in offense until she realized what had happened. "Don't go falling in love with it," he murmured in Grant's ear before using his slim might to release him into her.
