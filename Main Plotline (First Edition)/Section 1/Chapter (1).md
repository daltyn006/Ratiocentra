# Beskyddaren

The world's changed. Then again, it was inevitable. The streets were paved of the ash of the dead, which wrapped the nation with deep irreparable roots. Those who lived rushed over them proudly on their ways to the jobs they had no need for. The world is at one with the works of humanity and animal alike, organic and inorganic manipulated to benefit the whole. Grant was on his way to tutor under his father as a composer, of bringing him little joy. He would have much rather been away, doing nothing of importance, and still he respected his father enough to follow him to his office. The orange sun gloomed over the asphalt that would have spread greatly over the horizon if a small town had not been brutally slapped on the earth where the two rapidly approached. Their vehicle slowed automatically to merge and swim alongside the other sparce groups being moved to their destinations. The mirrors and windshield of the vehicle were completely complementary, no control was given to the users. If Grant had decided to look out towards the familiar buildings he gazed thoughtlessly upon for 23 years he would have instead seen a racoon and his family playing together through a window. Grant was more interested in the device which he softly held, hunched over, partially in his hand, mostly on his thigh. Intently he gazed at a dimly lit screen showing him images that scattered in his mind. He had set himself in an image of grandeur and glory, hundreds of people stared on at him as he simply danced, moves increasingly blended and blurred together like a heat mirage in the desert. Although the technology was not capable of audio, in his mind the applause and cheers almost brought him to be happy. The fluid motions now became more focused and flowing as a second member slowly formed on stage. Abruptly his focus was demolished with the unconscious leg bouncing which threw the screen off his leg and out of his grip to the floor, stopping abruptly like a rock and causing a loud thud. This would have drawn him attention if his father was not entranced in his own mind as well.

Their vehicle gradually came to a halt and released its prisoners to where they had set the location. The two stepped, barren, out the right side of the vehicle to the smooth limestone of the sidewalk. The soft gloss finish pale chalk white of the street provided a cool surface on which bare feet could stride handily without harm. As the sun rose slowly crept to watch the town, chalk white turned neon from the glow and illuminated any shadows or scary portions which could have hidden some monster. "Why does it need to always be so bright", Grant unthinkingly proclaimed annoyed that the glowing roads were no longer a safe place to set his hungover eyes. He already knew the answer, it was engrained in his mind from his current history course. Humanity moved to where they were stable. "You know the answer, have you been studying?", Mr. Pewdershmitt replied slightly concerned, "You know how important it is that you are prepared for your testing". "I am aware, I still don't see what this has anything to do with my rationality". Mr. Pewdershmitt did not see a reason to respond to that, it was clear and did not dignify an answer. As the two passed along the common architecture bold and blatant protruding from the otherwise bland gothic homes and businesses. Ahead stood a lion with his cubs staring at an extended verse Grant had read many times before. The two sauntered by slowly, as their pace slowed to enter their studio. The door was unlocked, as was every other door, and yet it was increasingly rare for anyone to mosey on in.

As per usual, the studio was empty. Only the instruments and computers bringing all the possibilities of the mind, body, and soul to life remained. Mr. Pedwdershmitt was never a gatekeeper, and often held sessions with all beings for inspiration and assistance with his prowess. Many transient people traveled through the small town of
Beskyddaren but few rarely stayed past the initial lull of the artistry. Nobody required labor anymore, and artistry was ever taken under critique as too difficult or uninteresting to create instead of enjoy. Especially since technology made it so easy to create, why resort to the older ways? These questions resided heavily in Grant, growing into an cacophony of sounds that quietly dissolved as he entered the control room after his father. "This is where the majority of my music is created," he started in a soft, warm, and eerily comfortable tone. Gesturing a wave to a small computer barely the size of a laptop the mid-sized monitor which spread across the wall flashed alive and unlocked to show where his music was made. Grant was not impressed, especially since the flash greatly blinded him from the sudden contrast between the moodily lit room and the bright white of the screen. As his eyes returned back to him, he could see text sprawling across the screen describing what sounded like a regular piece. "I normally start and end with this computer. Although it is imperative for the knowledge of instruments, adoptive testing, and theory to perfect the source, this is the source". With a grand, measured movement, Mr. Pewdershmitt turned to face the monitor and motioned huge orchestrating gestures that cued the computer to begin paying attention. Huge lines of thought flowed from him into the computer-- the refined gestures of ages of practice reflecting almost no difference or alterations between his desired output and the computer's. Once the song was complete he turned to his son, and gestured to go ahead.

"Start out by showing what you learned with the guitar. Remember, it is not the movements that need to be refined, but your mind. This is the difference between regular composers and the truly positioned ones. I may be able to create ballads of immense depth in minutes while it would take others decades of independent work. I expect that you remember your lessons at least." Mr. Pewdershmitt excitedly went up to the keyboard and changed around some settings. The air suddenly became thick with pressure. Although his father was always pleasurable to be around, the sudden spotlight set upon him was blinding, and the deep brown eyes distorted by thick spectacles transfixed him in his position. "Go ahead now boy, get a move on." He inserted gesturing intensely at the large border Grant was expected to stand within. Grant often listened to music in his mind and memorized what was existing, but could rarely think of anything original or creative. The moments he stood before the behemoth screen changed seconds to hours as he rambled through his mind to find anything worthy to show his father. One song stood out to him, it was the song that played when she entered. Soon enough, and before he even motioned, the computers thoughts refined and defined his perception of the song onto the speakers around him. While he rarely listened to what was produced by his father as being overly adjusted to the genre grew it to be plain, Grant had accidentally repeated the chords of his fathers friend, competitor, and sworn nemesis that had quietly been in the background from the speakers at the party last night.

"Impressive, I succumb that, but you truly could not have thought of a better work?" Mr. Pewdershmitt teased. "Still, that was not bad for a first song, the computer does most of the work- sure, but the refinement you carried and addition of the different instruments was more than expected. Well done!" The hours passed and the songs flew from their combined minds flourishing within the height of the home and expanding out into the roads around them. Grant had gotten used to the building by now, its wooden, dark coffee inside always smelt of cinnamon. The sound booth where they currently resided within had never crossed his mind as anything more complex than a large pantry, although there was no kitchen, and his past lessons had kept him so preoccupied that the sudden change of scenery in the place he was so accustomed to. As the clock slowly swam through the hours, it was soon midday and in happenstance lunchtime. Music could be continued later, as for now it was time for food.

Restaurants were always a highly regarded place. Groceries were no longer needed as all food was prepared by those that desired to make it, mostly robots. Meals were diverse and delicious set to what was desired in the mind, although some meats were no longer allowed to be consumed. The cozy restaurant down the road was a go to for the surrounding areas. It was said to be a university of the old age, but the combined water and electric between the four buildings and the central placement of the quad in the middle of town made it prime real estate for eating. While most people wanted to eat indoors in the air conditioning, Grant and his father sat in the outdoor area under the noon-day sun. Almost as soon as they arrived their meals were retrieved. Mr. Pewdershmitt desired some chicken, a usual course as farm birds rarely came into society. Grant had some lame. While people had become more rare in the world, those who lived were more closely knit within their travels. Lindesay Iverson was one of these travelers, who had happened in the same town as the two. As she entered the door she took a quick index of the room, looking for anyone familiar, quickly recognizing Mr. Pewdershmitt. "Fancy to see you here", she cooed as she sat down with the duo. "Where are you off to now?" Mr. Pewdershmitt inquired. Grant peered over his shoulder to see who was there and was instantly too flustered to speak. She had a warm and instantly recognizable face, her sparking diamond blue eyes and kept jet black hair drew attention away from the rest of her perfectly pale body. Her laugh filled Grant with a hopeful warmth, and the sound of her voice took any meaning out of it and instead directly touched his heart like a siren calling to a sailor. He had been attracted to her since 9th grade, and still in the 17th she had not lost any allure. His face slowly turned rosy as he became more flustered as she sat before him. "How are you faring with your preparations Grant?" she softly interrupted his inner thoughts, "You might have an extra year to prepare but things are no easier". "I've been practicing with the left side of my brain, tracking my sleep cycles to make sure the function is as good as possible. It's been really difficult with the pressure to have a genuinely clear mind while still knowing the truth" He responded, beginning to fidget with his hands, "I can't believe it's almost that time already, feels like yesterday we had our last tests." The memories of sitting inside of that machine loomed over the two, and it was nothing similar to the one upcoming. Mr. Pewdershmitt broke the concerning silence "You two will do fine, if I was able to make it through you both will. Everyone was concerned I wouldn't make it" "Thanks Mr. Pewdershmitt", Lindesay solemnly said more hushed and concerned than before, "My parents say the same thing". Grant stopped and solemnly back into his mind going directly to the thoughts of how she was too perfect for him and how if she failed the test nobody could get through. Silently as they ate Grant would glace into her eyes and see the blatant soulfulness within, innocent, caring, and bright, and then to his fathers, dull, dry, and animalistic.

Their meals finished and bellies full they departed. Lindesay and the rest hugged their goodbyes, getting disgruntled looks from the nearby porcupine family. Their walk back to the shop was nothing special, apart from one question from Grant's father "So... What did you think of Lindesay?" The blush on Grants face quickly returned at the mention of her name. The truth would come out eventually, and now would seem to have been an alright time admit it, "I actually met a girl actually in my league last night" he sheepishly replied, with the slight background of a boasting tone. "And you'd still pass up Lindesay?" His father playfully probed further. "Absolutely not!" Grant answered a little too fast, his heart exposing his mind "I mean, she is a very nice girl." "That and more..." his father replied, less of a joke this time "your mother would have loved her". The rest of the walk remained in silence, Mr. Pewdershmitt went back to thinking about what he would experiment on, and Grant kept his mind stuck on girls. As they approached the studio went over his mind over and over, comparing and contrasting the benefits and downsides, and the consequences.

The studio was noticeably colder than the rest of the outdoors, but the extra vitamins the sun provided were why almost nobody wore clothes in society. But the chilliness of the studio reminded him of the ski trip he took with both of his parents, the one which only one had come back. The trip to Colorado had been deemed 89% safe by the computers, but 7 years contains heavy advancement, and while the safety measurement was true, the computers that could help in a dangerous situation were lacking. Grant had been there, but as a young teenager with no medical training, he wouldn't have been capable of handling any medical situation, he was especially useless when she broke her leg three quarters up the mountain. She had always loved skiing after a fresh snowfall. That night, she was too eager. The rough ice stalking beneath the snow laid a trap for any skier unexpecting, the freezing conditions forced the drones into hiding, unable to help. As she slowly went down the ungroomed trail, the snow slid away revealing the ice beneath. While the two had been trained by their years of practice. Still, Grant had fell, and in her care his mother refused to let  him go all the way. 

 his mind schemed against him and slowly re-focused on the night prior.
