# Grant - Dream Sequence 1 

I watched the darkness smear my vision as I closed my eyes. The harsh whistling of the wind shrieked through the ponderosa pines drawing my mind back to the sound those machines had made as they probed my senses and threatened my life. Disregarding my experiences the wind remained persistent lulling me in; its wails clawing to fight my never ending insomnia- like the day my peers were massacred. The culling helped greatly with crime and humanities problems, and was soon expanded to wild beasts, death was at a new low. At least with those who survived. The Founders had figured a way to enter the mind and solve problems before they occurred. It's more innate knowledge than learned, my root capacities had to be filled by now. What is that at the corner of my eye? Who is at the door? Shadows danced along the ceiling stabbing at one another forcing the light to retreat back into any source emitting. The wind stopped, and I can feel them at the door. My flashlight shone on nothing, but laying back down it returned in the corner of my eye. There had to be someone there. Or is it a trick of my mind. thump- thump- thump-, my heart broke away from my arteries in the safety of my callous home. I was at home, but the notorious mechanical whirring spoke differently. The corner of my eyes filled with staticky artificial grey shadows as they slowly eased shut. A blinding bright white light forced its way through my eyes, the usual reds and whites burning and drying my retinas forced them open. A thin white dome surrounded my head and slowly wrapped around, wrapping and constricting like a venus fly trap around my brain. The stimulation of the senses made it impossible to resist being euthanized by the invisible needles that hid directly in front of my eyes. I could not see them but I knew they were there, animalistic senses forced me to fear. My lungs were hollow and could not be filled as the whirring stopped and the low bass took its place. Like a ship out on the sea being propelled with engines hidden underneath the stern, the course had been plotted and cannot be reverted. I forced myself back into my mind, I was sentient I know this, but I wasn't thinking- I couldn't think. So many before me did not think. The machine still smelt of the painless death that came with failing. The hum deepened and my breath slowed, I imagined those needles going into my brain, the quick and painless end I would meet. I should know soon. The heavenly lights broke away to reveal the conveyer belt leading me to freedom. It would be some time yet for me to exit, but at least I was alive. The mechanical whirr of the machinery barely drowned out the beginning of screams as the others around me felt tangs of pain before they were humanely executed. My head was released from its holds and I looked back to where I had been. A woman was following me, her blonde hair and hazel eyes were drawn straight ahead. I had not noticed how close to death I was, the needles were mere centimeters from my eyes. Her chest rapidly expanded and contracted, her eyes darted up to see the alien device descending upon her to decide her fate. Instead, in a vain attempt to flee, she decided to take fate into her own hands. She lurched her head forward ignorant of the danger immediately in front of her. The blood spurted from her optic nerve a sudden wash of brilliant scarlet flooded the white of the eye, stark and violent against the pale skin of her face. We were given one rule to follow. Through her dismay, she rapidly brought her hands to protect her eyes from danger, breaking her grip with the kill switches. In doing so, her restraints were released and she was let go. They were problems. All of them. I had no reason to feel for her. I let the conveyer take me further down, but this was not the right way. No- none of this is what happened. I fell.

I opened my eyes to see a most beautiful view, a half full glass of amber whiskey. I had landed in my leather arm chair, friends surrounding me, celebrating our success of our test. "
