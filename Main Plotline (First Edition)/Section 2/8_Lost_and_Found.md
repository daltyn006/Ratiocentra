# Lost and Found

As Grant awoke, he wondered why he hadn’t just submitted to the Match System like his father. It might have saved him from the pain—the quiet, dragging torture he had put himself through. But he was whole now. He and Manada were whole now—or at least, she was. Over the last few days they’ve spent together, her spirits had brightened as their rhythms aligned. He used to count his moments with Lindesay because he lived under the shadow of their end. Now, he had nowhere else to be and an infinity to get there. Yet he remained utterly helpless: just as a drone drops from the sky when its battery cuts, he found himself falling for Manada in every small moment they shared. It hadn't taken long after she nursed him back to health for the old group's hangout spot to regain its pull, coming back to life with a quiet, easy warmth.

With their examinations approaching, the rest of the circle had finally wised up and settled down. Though citizens were allowed to use the Match System at any point, most held out hope of discovering their soulmate in the wild before the mandatory testing date; few ever did, usually opting in the end to let the algorithm hand them their perfect partner. Now, each of the new women who sat in the lounge either beside or on their spouses possessed a distinct, impossible sublimity. And while the core of their sessions had shifted from light, trivial banter to meaningful games and psychological prompts, the bond between them remained untouched. Eira had matched with Anderson long before he ever thought to check the system, and it showed. The dynamic chemistry that shifted like tectonic plates deep beneath their feet always stayed seamlessly in tune. Her dark brown eyes, soft curves, and easy, motherly presence grounded Anderson's broad, heavy frame as he kept his arm draped over her. Eira was perhaps the only other person in their region who insisted on wearing her craft, her leatherworking skills displayed in the exquisite fur boots she wore to match Anderson's own.

The most friction anyone had ever witnessed between them was a petty, laughing argument over who could deliver a punchline louder. To the cold world outside, their shared look was a barrier against any storm. They didn't need to touch to stay warm; the steady, unblinking devotion in their eyes was its own log fire, holding them both safe against the bitter white expanse. Across the room, Gisela was trading travel stories with Manada. Her pea-less wooden whistle that hung over her sternum matched Christoph's, their shared love for extreme terrain drawing them together. Gisela had actually met Christoph years before the system ever paired them, back when she was navigating the backcountry.

"I'm telling you, he nearly gave me a heart attack," Gisela said, leaning back with a laugh and gesturing with her drink. "Five years ago. I’m walking the northern ridge trail, and out of nowhere, this young ranger steps straight out of the brush right in front of me, staring at the dirt like he's measuring the earth."

"I was surveying the soil composition," Christoph interjected quietly, his voice calm and precise as he leaned against the armrest of her chair. "You didn't even look at me!" Gisela countered, smiling. "Then, two miles and three hours later, I pull off my pack to rest by the creek. I wake up, and there he is again, standing three feet away. And that time, he looked right at me and let out this sweet, soft little smile."

"I was smiling at a rabbit," Christoph said, his usual stoic posture faltering just enough for a faint flush to hit his neck. "It was behind you in the brush." "It was not a rabbit, Christoph," Gisela laughed, nudging him with her shoulder. "You were smitten." "The rabbit had very... distinct markings," he maintained, though the corner of his mouth twitched upward.

Near the corner of the lounge sat Desdemona. She was noticeably older than the rest, at least in her posture and expression; and ran a nervous hand through her flowing red hair, her stylish curls catching the amber light. Her forest-green eyes had a permanent dark weight beneath them, the look of someone kept awake either by Daneder or by the heavy sedatives she quietly ingested while the rest of the group enjoyed the company.

Daneder sat beside her with a posture that was a little too straight, his slicked hair and sharp posture radiating a performative confidence. Desdemona’s attention rarely left him; every few minutes, she would lean in and softly ask his permission for the smallest things- to grab a drink, to shift her chair, to speak. It was an unsettling sight to the rest of the room, made stranger by the fact that whenever Daneder flatly told her no, she quietly thanked him for it. "We're heading out," Daneder announced abruptly, cutting off a conversation he hadn't been part of. He didn't wait for Desdemona to gather her coat before standing. She scrambled to follow, offering a tight, apologetic smile to the room. They barely made it through the heavy door before the lounge seamlessly closed the gap, the remaining group letting the strange tension melt away.

A moment later, a wet, sudden thud echoed from the balcony above, followed by a heavy splash on the patio stones below- barely missing the overhang where Daneder and Desdemona had walked out seconds prior. Everyone froze. From over the second-floor railing, Beorme's pale face popped up, his dark hair hanging in his eyes and his scabby wrists clinging to the wooden bar. He looked down at the mess on the lower patio, then at the empty walkway where the couple had just been standing.

"Ah, damn," Beorme wheezed, breaking into a cackling, breathless laugh that made his hunched shoulders shake. "Missed 'em." "Beorme, you absolute mad-lad," Anderson called up, shaking his head with a grin while Eira hid her face in his shoulder to muffle her laughter. "You almost took them out, and not in a good way!"

"Would've improved their dynamic," Beorme muttered, wiping his mouth with the back of his hand. He looked around at the paired-off couples sitting in the warm glow of the room, his chaotic grin softening into something small and distant. He leaned his chin on his forearms over the railing. "Alright, that's it. It's too crowded down there with all this love in the air. I'm going to the center to sign up for a match tomorrow." "You? Matched?" Gisela teased, looking up. "Is the algorithm ready for that?" "It better be," Beorme said, flashing a crooked, pale smile before disappearing back into the upstairs shadow. "I need someone to clean up after my terrible aim."

Beorme's footsteps faded down the hall and down the stairs, leaving a comfortable, lingering hum of laughter in the room. Anderson shook his head, taking a slow sip of his drink, while Eira adjusted the wool blanket over their knees. Grant watched the alcohol dance in his cup, his eyes drifting out toward the fogged window that separated the warm lounge from the vast, silent grid outside. The casual mention of signing up for a match was still hanging in the air, stirring a quiet question that had been sitting heavy in his chest. "How do you think it actually works?" Grant asked softly.

The room didn't go silent, but the easy rhythm of the conversation dipped, gears turned in their minds slower than regular from the impairment. Christoph glanced over from the armrest of Gisela's chair, his brow furrowing slightly and gaze drifting off to the wall behind, unfocused and deep in thought as he attempted to decipher his question. "How does what work?" "The matches," Grant said, turning his glass in his hands. "The testing. The pairing. Everyone just goes into a subterranean room, goes asleep for a year, and comes out with a fresh new perspective. But nobody actually knows what the system measures down there. Nobody knows how it calculates us."

Anderson let out a low, amused huff- the classic response of a man who saw no point in picking at a smooth surface to look for cracks. "It calculates compatibility, Grant. Personality traits, neural responses, lifestyle habits. It's an algorithm. A damn good one, considering it put Eira in front of me." He nudged his wife, who offered a warm, agreeing smile, though her eyes lingered on Grant with a subtle, observant concern. "I know what it claims to do," Grant pushed gently, his tone curious rather than aggressive. "I mean how. Who wrote the baseline? How does it work? We live in these sectors, we eat what the transit grids deliver, we get matched when the clock runs out, and nobody ever stops to look at the machinery running beneath the floorboards." Christoph shifted, his posture straightening into his usual orderly, pragmatic stance. His resistance wasn't hostile—just the precise logic of someone who trusted functional design. "Does the mechanism matter if the result is stable?" Christoph asked, keeping his voice even. "You're asking for the schematic of a watch when all you need to do is tell the time, Grant. The grid maintains the sectors, the match protocol minimizes social friction, and we get to sit in a warm room in the middle of winter. Why look for a mystery?"

"I'm just curious about how it works," Grant murmured. "It is when it keeps you alive and fed out here," Christoph countered lightly, his tone remaining friendly. "You spend too much time staring into the dark, just relax."

"He's not wrong to wonder, though," Gisela chimed in lightly, breaking the rising tension before it could harden. She rested a hand on Christoph's knee, giving him a playful look. "Even if you did think you were smiling at a rabbit, Christoph, the system still put you back on my trail. It's a little eerie if you think about it too long. Brilliant, but eerie." "It's just math, nothing maleficent behind it," Christoph maintained, though he let out a small sigh and leaned back, conceding the topic for the sake of peace. Manada, who had been sitting quietly beside Grant, reached out and brushed her knuckles against his arm. She didn't argue with Christoph or defend Grant's paranoia; she simply grounded him, her presence a quiet reminder of the world right in front of him. "The world outside is big, Grant," Manada said softly, her amber eyes reflecting his face, "And it's been quiet for a very long time. Maybe people just stopped asking questions because they were afraid of what the answers would demand of them." Grant watched the room for a moment, the interest he sowed had grew to fruition, and the others were thinking about it. 

After a while and the conversations returned to their normal status and the booze kept flowing. Topics thrown and buried. Across the table, Christoph didn't look up from where he was checking the brass casing of his pea-less whistle.  Grant went on to say much later and much more intoxicated, "Well whoever started this up is long dead, there's no harm in checking out what's around us." "He's right," Anderson added, his heavy hand resting on the back of Eira's chair, his handmade leather boots settled firmly against the hearthstone. "We should go on an adventure." 

"Where? To the archives?" Manada murmured, her voice cool and precise from the corner of the bench. She met Grant's gaze, her dark eyes holding his with that familiar, unbending intensity. "What archives?" Grant returned to her, "Records. If they exist anywhere, they aren't in the older archives or the Hasseoka." Grant frowned, his eyes settling still on hers. "But, there might be some in Hasseoka" The name hung in the warm air like draft frost. Grant rarely had reason to venture into Luxuria- the upper sector was a place of ostentatious comfort and guarded privacy, far removed from the functional rhythm of his daily routines. "There's nothing in Luxuria except Daneder's den, bars and private estates," Grant said, his mind rapidly working through the geography of a district he barely knew. "That's what evolved around the modern archives," Manada replied softly playing into his interest. 

They began to play some games, setting the monitor to mirror their drunken minds. While it started as a bad game of Pictionary, it dissolved very quickly into a series of horrific scenes. The machine  caught the heavy, warm- tense current running through the room. Each lover took their turn thinking about their turn "drawing" their spouse, the screen broadcasting their desire in vivid, lingering detail. Intimate, shameless snapshots of physical devotion flashed for the whole group to see: the weight of a hand pressed against the small of a back, the curve of a neck exposed to the firelight, the deep, instinctive pulse of two people who knew every inch of each other's bodies. It rotated like this a few times, the room thick with rowdy laughter, playful teasing, shared glances, and the easy warmth of alcohol, until people started to run out of ideas.

The machine caught the heavy, warm current running through the room. Each pair took their turn thinking about "drawing" their partner, the screen broadcasting their desire in vivid, lingering detail. Intimate, shameless snapshots of physical devotion flashed for the whole group to see, the perception only a lover should have, the weight of a heavy thigh thrown over a hip, the curve of an exposed throat offered up to the firelight, and the deep, instinctive pulse of bodies pressed tight in absolute abandon of embarrassment. It rotated like this a few times, the lounge thick with rowdy laughter, playful teasing, sloshing mugs, and the easy, uninhibited warmth of alcohol, until people started to run out of ideas.

Then, it was Grant's turn. Maybe it was the lingering strain that even Manada's smooth, bare skin pressed against his side couldn’t entirely erase. Maybe it was the surreal pressure and beauty of all these faces filling the lounge. Maybe it was just the dreariness of the long, freezing night pressing against the cabin walls. Grant rested his hands on the console, his head heavy from the drink. The machine hummed as his mind tried to latch onto the game, intending to draw Manada's necklace that inexplicitly laid right above her breasts, but the screen didn't capture a drawing- it pulled an unwilling, unbidden memory that thrashed out of his drunken subconscious and spilled into the light.

The illicit, fiery imagery on the monitor instantly died, replaced by something hauntingly still, a simple change that sucked the breath right out of the room. The projected scene blurred and shuddered with the haze of intoxication, two separate horrors melting together in his mind. It was Lindesay. She was standing at the far end of what seemed to be a factory floor, past the final machine, surrounded by damp concrete and heavy shadows. Her hair hung loose down her back. But her hands were not empty; she was cradling a small, swaddled shape against her chest with intense concentration as tears streamed down her face. An overwhelming grief that had her bowed under its weight.

The screen zoomed in on them, approaching them, walking past the grinding machinery toward her, the voice projecting in his mind not showing through on the screen, cracking into pieces as he tried to apologize for what he didn't know. But as he reached out, the memory distorted in the drunken connection. A flash of heat washed over the screen. The small body in her arms blackened and burned, the char spreading up Lindesay's arms and consuming her whole until both mother and child were reduced to a dark, smoldering remnant tumbling away on an unseen belt. Grant's projected self stared directly at it without seeing it, his face totally blank, completely oblivious to what he was looking at. The projection held long enough for everyone in the lounge to see the agonizing reality of it; perplexed by the bizarre factory setting and the sudden presence of a infant, before snapping back into pitch black as Grant violently closed his eyes and started to shake.

"I'm sorry," he muttered, his voice rough, staring down at his bare knees in the sudden, heavy stillness. The silence didn't last long, though the wild energy in the room flattened completely into soft, casual warmth. Manada didn't pull away. Her eyes stayed fixed on Grant's profile, watching the subtle tremor in his jaw, the hard set of his shoulders, and the sudden rigidity of his spine. A quiet, protective stillness settled over her. She knew nothing of any factory, nor why a child had appeared in his mind, but the raw terror in his posture was unmistakable. Instead of shifting back, she shifted closer, sliding her arm around his waist, resting her forehead against his shoulder, and pressing her hand firmly over his chest, feeling the frantic beat of his heart to anchor him back into the room.

Across the table, Anderson broke the hush with a soft, slurred chuckle, waving a lazy hand through the air to brush off the weirdness of the projection. "Hey, don't apologize for having good taste, brother. She was a beauty," he murmured, grinning through his heavy, drunken haze before reaching for his mug. Eira leaned her head onto Anderson's broad shoulder, resting a hand gently on his bare knee. She offered Grant a light, easy giggle, her eyes darting between him and Manada with warm admiration. "Honestly, Grant... your mind makes up some strange stuff when you're loaded, but it just shows you've got a good heart. Nothing wrong with that."

Beside them, Christoph set his glass down on the wood with a quiet, solid thud. He didn't laugh. His clear blue eyes stayed grounded on Grant, his posture leaning forward slightly, unsettled by the surreal dark turns Grant's mind was taking. Gisela, sitting close against Christoph's side, reached across the table and lightly brushed her fingers over the back of Grant's hand. "He's right, Grant," she said softly, her voice earnest and serious amidst the drunken fog. "It's a strange memory, but she meant a lot to you. You're allowed to hold onto her." Christoph nodded slowly, his arm resting over Gisela's back as he spoke, his voice low and steady. "It takes a lot to carry memories like that, even the ones that don't make sense. Don't beat yourself up, Grant."

Manada's fingers tightened gently against Grant’s ribs, her thumb tracing small, soothing circles into his skin. She didn't look at the screen where the projection had died; her gaze remained entirely on him, observing every micro-expression, every tight breath, studying the mysterious grief he kept locked away. She leaned in close, her warm breath catching against his neck as she whispered softly, "See? No harm done. I've got you." Anderson reached for the bottle, sloppily pouring another generous splash into Grant's glass and sliding it across the timber with a lopsided grin. "To good women, past and present. Drink up, Grant!"

Grant took the drink Anderson slid over, swallowing a mouthful of the warm liquid as it burned its way down his throat. Across the table, the couples settled back into each other, the momentary dark blip of his projected memory fading like smoke in the quiet hearth-light. Anderson pulled Eira close against his broad chest, her quiet laughter muffled into his neck, while Christoph traced slow, gentle patterns over Gisela’s back as she rested her head against his shoulder. The room returned to its effortless, uninhibited warmth, comfortable in their shared nudity and the easy hum of alcohol.

Beside him, Manada didn't shift away. Her amber eyes held a quiet, watchful heat as she turned her body toward his. Her bare knee slid against the outside of his thigh, and her hand moved down from his ribs, fingers lightly trailing across his abdomen before settling lower, her touch deliberate and intoxicating. She leaned in, her throat exposed to the firelight, her soft lips brushing against the shell of his ear as her fingers gently coaxed him, anchoring his mind back into his body, into her. She wanted him here—present, breathing, and hers.

Grant’s breath caught in his throat. His blood ran hot under her touch, but even as his hands instinctively moved to shape the curve of her hip, his drunken mind began to spin off its axis. The warmth of her skin under his hands, the sheer, undeniable reality of her holding him, stirred an unexpected, hazy ache in his chest. He thought of children—the ghost of the tiny, swaddled shape that had just burned on the monitor, the life he and Manada could build, the weight of a lineage he was supposed to carry.

And with that thought came his father's voice, unbidden and echoing through the booze-fogged darkness of his mind. I want to hold what comes out of you before I stop getting to. I want the line to go on. His father, sitting alone in that empty room, talking about the four grandchildren he didn't have, talking about grief like it was something you could just choose to put down. Grant leaned his head back against the bench, letting out a slurred, breathless laugh that broke through Manada's quiet coaxing. "He thinks he knows everything," Grant muttered into the warm air.

Manada paused, her fingers lingering against his skin, her dark eyes looking up at him with a mix of curiosity and soft frustration as he resisted her pull. "Who?" she asked softly. "My old man," Grant said, his head heavy, his eyes turning toward the dark window that faced out into the frozen grid. "Talking about the line. Talking about matches and legacy like it's all written down somewhere." He turned his head back to her, his hand sliding up to rest against her bare waist, his fingertips stroking her skin as he tried to trace the thread of his own drunken logic. "You said Luxuria... you said the modern archives evolved around Daneder's sector."

"They did," Manada murmured, her voice smooth and patient as she leaned her chest against his arm, still trying to draw his focus back to the bench, back to the fire. "Records of baseline matches, census data, lineage tracking. But nobody looks at them, Grant." "Why trek to Luxuria, then?" Grant said, a loose, lopsided grin slipping across his face as the alcohol spun his thoughts into a half-cocked conspiracy. "If it's modern, it's all on the grid. I don't need to walk out into the cold. I can pull up the baseline records right here." Across the hearth, Anderson let out a low, rumbling laugh, running a broad hand over Eira's back. "Leave it to Grant to try and hack the registry while sitting half-sloshed in front of a fire."

"Watch," Grant slurred, chuckling as he rested his hands on Manada, and looked at the screen, letting his impaired, floating thoughts lock onto the screen's visual feed. "If my father's so sure about how many grandchildren he's supposed to have, let's see what the system actually registered under his name. Let's see how smart the match algorithm really is." Manada let out a quiet sigh, but her smile was fond as she stayed close, her bare body pressed against his side, her chin resting on his shoulder to watch the monitor hum to life.

Grant's unfocused neural pulse pushed into the network. The screen flickered, smoothing out from the previous game’s raw, sensory shapes into the stark, clean lines of the system's census registry. Text scrolled down in rapid, luminous bands: GRENZER, W. — RESIDENTIAL & REPRODUCTIVE ARCHIVE.

Grant kept his playful grin ready, waiting to laugh off whatever dry, ancient data popped up to satisfy his drunken curiosity. He thought maybe he'd find some old match application, or some hidden file about his mother. But as his neural pulse navigated past his own birth entry, the system auto-filled an adjacent branch on the family tree—a medical and lineage record tagged directly under his father’s identity from twenty-four years ago.

The screen paused on a single, unmistakable entry:

LINEAGE RECORD: GRENZER, F.

OFFSPRING: 2

SUBJECT 1: GRANT GRENZER (CONFIRMED)

SUBJECT 2: BESRA (CONFIRMED - MATERNAL SEPARATION / HALF-SISTER)

STATUS: LIVE BIRTH / UNLINKED MATCHING / SEPARATED AT DELIVERY

Grant’s joking mood died instantly. The loose grin froze on his face.

His eyes locked onto the glowing letters, scanning them once, twice, three times. The slurred chuckle in his throat completely dried up. The drunken fog in his head didn't clear, but it froze solid.

A half-sister. A girl born under his father's line, carried by another woman, completely unmentioned by his father, completely erased from his entire life.

Beside him, Manada’s touch shifted. Sensing the sudden, terrifying shift in his posture, her playful arousal vanished, replaced instantly by hyper-vigilant concern. She felt the sudden halt in his breathing, the way his spine locked like iron beneath her hands.

She shifted her gaze from his face to the monitor, her dark eyes quickly scanning the stark lines of text before snapping back to his pale, rigid face. She didn't say a word. Her hand slid down his waist to grip his wrist firmly, her warm thumb pressing against his pulse point, observing the violent, erratic spike in his heart rate as he stared at the screen, entirely unable to breathe.
