# Recreation

The cool breeze touched Grant's nape as he lain on the soft mattress- a known touch, Lindesay's touch, wrapped him as he woke- and without opening his eyes- he hugged back tightly, breathing in her warmth. But her smell was not the same, it was clean and sterile- a hint of faded ink landed on his lips. She must have recently been in the darkroom. Grant pulled himself up- or her down- to get a better hold around her, but the softness of the mattress dissipated wildly as he touched the cold floor, why were they lying on the floor? He must have fell asleep in a bad position and pinned her underneath him, but he knew she wouldn't mind if he held her a little longer. He moved up to allow a better hold, and as the light in the room did not waver- Grant's mind did, slowly wandering- slithering back into him as he awoke.

The examination.

Grant opened his eyes to not see Lindesay's smiling face returning to him, but Manada's. Her face was unnerving to see- her hair had partially grown back into a short bob, ink still lining her face, the worst part in Grant's mind, was that it was comforting. Her smile was beautiful. Her diamond eyes and lips shown pure attraction- and the blush on her cheeks had abandoned any sensibility Grant had. He stared in emotional confusion at the view before him. The woman he longed for over many years- had refused others in hope for- was holding him tightly, and shyly smiling pressed beneath him. Then, he noticed the necklace around her neck. The emotions flowed through him, bursting through the dam he had tried to set once more, but this time he allowed her to leave. He had no use for it anymore. Manada stopped him as he walked out of the center and over to the grass, not being able to find any words to say, she instead walked right in front of him and launched her arms around his right before he collapsed. She fell with him again, but Grant shifted just in time to protect her from the descent- instead taking both the blow from the ground and her head against his chest. "Why... " Grant sobbed, "Why are you still here?" Manada did not have any words to say. "Why are you wearing that necklace?" This time she had reason, "It felt right, it felt right to be like you, In your reflection, I found the softest version of myself." The tears stopped flowing, and although he had no idea on how long Lindesay had been gone, some resemblance of hope returned- repairing- resembling a clogged artery in his heart that stopped the bleeding. 

The roles had reversed, and she was now pinning him- not with grief but with love. An affection worthy of nobody that set him back in his place. The tears stopped flowing and his eyes dried enough to fully see her against the pasty clouds. The expression she gave him was indescribable, although both had been too coy to interact in full, something had changed within her- and the fairness she displayed overwhelmed his mind and his heart which leapt out of his chest with every beat. Manada felt his heart begin to race, and with both of their faces flushing with something that had always meant to be. His mind and heart brought him to regain his sanity, to know Lindesay would want her to be happy, she would have wanted that, right?

They kissed. 

Manada, noticing the change of mood, rose to her feet- exhibiting as much as possible to cause chase. Passion swelled between the two, Grant had not known the forests that Manada had become so accustomed to, but the longing in his heart and the desperation for something whole to return to him brought him to follow closely after her. Once the two had reached deep into the wilderness away from the pavement that any rare vehicle passed would be inaudible- Manada had stopped running, only to be caught and warned. "Don't you see them?" Grant's eyes were too wide open, too red from the tears, too full of emotion- desperation- and now, a new emotion, fear. Fear that caused her to cling to his arm as she looked around frantically. She might have continued her flight if anything had been around them. The memories of thousands of hands grasping at her- including his, was still fresh within her mind. And that is exactly what Grant was seeing. The shadows had been looming patiently in the woods not daring to cross the roads and instead opting to occupy every space between root and limb. For some reason, Manada ran right into their residence without any warning, running into and splitting the forms into howling figures that spread the skies and angrily swirled above her head. Manada responded after a moment. "See what?" The overcast that shrouded her was steeped in her light, and the figures were gone. Manada had not noticed any danger, but she did notice something else. She laid down on nearby moss, and salaciously imposed herself between him and his illusions. Her words were truth when she said, "You're safe".
