Her coyness returned when the doctor began to gently mark her head, one hand gently placed to say it's alright, the other moving with dedication to complete the task. A set of needles descended on her, and before she could react she was taken to her room.

I awoke in a state of ignorant bliss to see a digital room around me, blinding blue rays and deep purples tracing the corners of the rooms. Only, they didn't stop at each corner, they continued down in a long descending tunnel that drew me towards its end. A low rumbling, distant at first, rose from the blackness. I thought to run, but an impenetrable wall forced me back on my ass. I noticed the light pulsated with my every step and that the tunnel had turns and twists that forced me to slow and hold my hands out ahead as the low rumbling rose, surrounding me at all angles. The tunnel did not end, but instead looped back on itself- and the sound rose like applause into a grand ovation and began to reverberate through bone and body. A sound that lifted me from the tunnels, lifted me from the blue and purple tunnels that trapped me, and jostled me blind as if swimming in the challenger deep vibrating me until my limbs went numb and I knew that- nothing would happen. The sound had stopped. The entity had left me alone. Where am I? Slowly I felt something was wrong, like I was forgetting something terrible. I searched my mind to find nothing. One singular speck of white in the distance rapidly approached me like a comet. This was not the only one, looking around there were many distant specks approaching me. They were here.

Hyperventilating, I saw figures approach me from all angles. The colors of their skin were wrong. Their pigmentations were correct, but what were they wearing? Where was I? The people existed but the whites of the walls did not exist. They were carrying simple rectangles in their hands, and ovals on their backs. I stood there, trembling, my fear a roaring dragon inside me, its breath hot and searing, its claws digging into my ribs. Every part of me was screaming, but the fear inside me had already taken root, its tendrils wrapping around my thoughts, pulling me deeper into the abyss of my own mind.  

I sat on the cold bench in the long tunnel, my unclothed form prickling against the damp breath of the place. The walls stretched endlessly, lined with sputtering lights that painted everything in washed-out bone and silver ash, interrupted only by the charcoal of shadows and the umber of abandoned cases stacked like shed skins. I knew not how I had arrived, nor what this passage was named. I knew only the tongue in my mind and the motion of my limbs. Around me, figures drifted; countless souls in wool and linen, bearing leather satchels, flowing past with gazes fixed on impossible horizons. They did not see me. They did not see each other. They simply moved like water around the stone of my presence, I who sat unveiled, my soft architecture exposed to the stale currents.

I rose.

The change was absolute. Every face turned toward me as if pulled by threads from above, cogs that started to turn in an ancient machine. A man in grey halted mid-stride, his case suspended in time. A woman turned, hands falling empty to her sides. A child lifted his face from nothing, his eyes suddenly burning with ancient hunger. Everyone stood and stared at me, I knew what they were thinking, I saw it in their eyes, three overlapping terrifying sounds repeated in my mind until they words, three words, "Let us in.". I stood still in fear. The sound subsided, and with subtle minutes of wondering coming to pass, and I decide to sit back down- hopeful they would continue without noticing me. They surged forward in silence. Hands found me- rough palms instantly pressing on mine, fingers seeking everywhere, probing unasked the entirety of my body with insistent need and curiosity. Others grasped me from behind, full hands exploring, searching, seeking to know me. I tried to cry out but the mass of hands pressed against my face. Through the impossible, pressing mass, through tears and terror, I saw him.

He stood upon the metal ribbons in the distance, he was also naked, his pale form taut and ghostly befitting the scenery. He did not move. He simply watched, arms hanging at his sides, his gaze that of the wolf who has found the wounded deer, the angler fish luring with light. Waiting. Hunting. He was the only one like me- bare in the depths, unveiled, real in a way the others were merely shadow. I feared him, feared the starvation in his eyes, yet I pressed forward anyway, climbing upon the shoulders of the mass, my bare soles pressing against faces and grasping hands that sought to pull me back into their embrace. They clawed at my ankles, my calves, my thighs. Someone's tongue pressed against me as I crawled on top of the horde, warm and insistent, before I kicked free. I scaled the wall of bodies, hands clutching my chest, fingers removing all boundaries, but I kept ascending, kept striving, my gaze locked on the man who watched me as though I were the meal he had waited lifetimes to consume.

I reached the edge and leaped, landing hard on the stones between the metal ribbons. The crowd released me instantly—as if some thread had been cut. They stepped back, blinking, unphased at what they had just done. The man in grey consulted his timepiece. The woman smoothed her coat. They resumed their drifting flow, their eyes fixed ahead, as if the previous moments had been merely dream. None looked at me. None looked at the ribbons. I laid there on the tracks, exhausted from the exertion- marked by the passage of hands, my throat tender from screams I forgot erupted from me, skin bearing the prints of a hundred fingers, facing the unclothed man who had waited. He moved toward me with slow, deliberate steps, his gaze never wavering. When he reached me, he extended his hand, not in gentleness- but in command. I took it. His grip was iron, painful, as he pulled me up from the stones to stand before him on the metal path. I trembled in his arms, my unveiled form pressed to his, feeling his body against my own- hot and insistent against my belly.

"Who am I..." I began, my voice cracking, desperate, the question I had carried since first consciousness in this place. "Who am I to be loved?" He permitted no more. His lips sealed mine, cutting the words short, and he lowered me to the cold metal ribbon. I struggled beneath his weight, hands pushing against his chest, legs kicking, but he was immovable, relentless. The sensation was blinding, absolute, terror flooding my veins as he began to move with mechanical, predatory precision, his eyes locked on mine, drinking my fear, feeding on my resistance. I writhed beneath him, hands beating against his unyielding form, limbs seeking escape, but he was stone, relentless. I was going to fracture, I was going to dissolve, I was-

The tunnel flickered.

The bone-whites and silver-ashes strobed, blinding azure shafts cutting through the dimness. The walls dissolved at the edges of my sight, pixelating, melting like frost in sudden spring. The crowd vanished entirely, leaving only empty air where they had drifted. The man above me remained, but his face shifted, softened, the wolf's hunger transforming into something else, something warm and welcoming as morning.

I looked up at him, and suddenly I was not afraid. The pain transmuted into connection, into the warmth of being held after ages of solitude. The metal beneath my back was no longer cold; it was the firmness of reality asserting itself. His movements were no longer invasion; they were embrace, the only language that could reach me through the walls I had built around my heart. The long tunnel was breaking apart, the antique aesthetic dissolving into blinding white radiance. I knew not where I was. I knew not who I was. But I knew, suddenly, in the deepest chamber of my mind, that something had changed. I opened to his embrace like a flower to rain, accepting him. As the reality shattered completely around us, I realized I was weeping. Not from terror, but from the sweet relief of finally being found.

I wake with my cheek against moss that smells like copper and rotting veal, and I remember everything. The tunnel. The hands. The man with the brown eyes who touched me like I was something precious. The test isn't over—it's changed.

The woods are different now. Before, they were gray and hesitant. Now the green is too vivid, pulsing like infected tissue. I can feel the air pressing against my skin, eager to be let in. I know what I did wrong in the last place. I was closed. I was afraid. I won't make that mistake again.

I see him standing between two birch trees.

He's tall, six feet maybe, with a square jaw that looks like it was carved from something stubborn. His hair is brown, swept to one side in a way that suggests he once cared about his appearance but has forgotten why. His eyes are brown too- warm, but with a depth that suggests he's seen things he can't unsee. His skin is wrong, though. Scabbed, scarred, ragged across his knuckles and jawline, the marks of someone who has fallen and kept falling. He looks hungry. Not just for food, for contact. For someone to stop his freefall. Our eyes meet, and the world tilts.

I walk toward him, and he walks toward me, and the distance between us collapses faster than it should. We don't speak. We don't need to. I can feel his grief already, radiating off him like heat from a fever. He's lost someone. He's lost himself. I want to be the thing that finds him. When we touch, it's not like before. In the tunnel, the touch was a question. This is an answer that comes too fast, too absolute. Our hands clasp, and the sweat between our palms doesn't evaporate- it thickens. Fibrous. Vascular. I feel his pulse in my fingertips, but it's not his pulse anymore. It's ours. Capillaries reach across the gap like tendrils, knitting our skin together with threads of pink tissue that pulse and weep clear fluid. I should pull back, but it feels like sinking into warm water. His heartbeat is in my chest now, loud and insistent, beating in sync with mine until I can't tell which is which.

We kiss, and our lips don't separate. The mucous membranes fuse with a wet, organic sound that should disgust me but instead feels like relief. Finally, I think. Finally, someone who won't leave. Our tongues touch, and the muscle fibers intertwine, literal knots forming under the surface, anchoring us together. I taste his saliva mixing with mine, but it's not mixing; it's becoming a third substance, thick and ropey, binding our mouths in a seal that won't break. We are holding each other, and then we are merging. His chest opens like a flower; not with violence, but with a wet, yielding separation of flesh. The skin peels back in petals, revealing the red muscle beneath, the white fascia, the glint of ribs. I press into him, or he presses into me- I can't tell the direction anymore. Our ribcages interlace, bone scraping against bone with a sound like teeth grinding on chalk, until they lock together like fingers clasping in prayer. I can feel his liver working, filtering blood that flows through my veins now too, his hepatic tissue pulsing against my diaphragm. His lungs expand, and my lungs expand with them, drawing air we share, his alveoli touching mine through the membrane where our pleural cavities have fused.

I am not me anymore. I am we.

At first, it's ecstasy. I know him completely. I know the weight of his mother's loss, the phantom coldness he carries in his chest, the way he hears metal scraping against his spine when he's alone. I know that he clings to his pain because it's the only proof he's human. I love him for it. I love him with a ferocity that scorches. But then comes the hour, maybe the second hour, maybe the tenth, when I want to scratch an itch on my shoulder, and the arm won't move because he doesn't feel it, and his indifference becomes my paralysis. I have a thought. I want to keep something private, just one small memory of my own. He hears it through our shared nervous system, his neurons firing in response before I've finished thinking it. He reacts to it, his sadness flooding my synapses before I can choose my own emotion. I feel his boredom during what should be my pleasure, a dull, gray static that overrides my arousal. I feel his stray, intrusive thoughts about food, about sleep, about the way my hair smells wrong to him, sour and human, and his irritation becomes my self-loathing before I can defend against it. There is no interiority. There is no self that is only mine.

I start to hate him.

Not because he's cruel. He loves me with a devotion that would be worship if it weren't so hungry. I hate him because he's there, always, in every thought, like a song stuck in my head that I can't stop humming. I hate the way his grief weighs on my lungs, his depression making my chest tight, my breath shallow. I hate the scars on his skin that I can feel from the inside, tender and raised, the scabs pulling with every movement we make together. I hate that I can feel his hunger in my own stomach, gnawing, the acid building up in our shared gut. I hate that when he remembers his mother, I taste copper and salt in the back of our throat. I hate that I can't remember what my own heartbeat sounds like without his on top superseding it, arrhythmic and wrong.

I want out.

I push against him, and our fused flesh resists. We have grown together. Shared skin, shared blood, shared nerves. Separating is not a pulling apart. it's tearing. The sound is like wet paper ripping, like Velcro made of meat, like a sodden book being pulled apart page by page. Blood sprays and we are screaming in harmony, and the pain is exquisite, biblical, the pain of birth run in reverse. Our interlaced ribs grind as we pull, bone scraping bone, the cartilage tearing with wet pops. Our shared diaphragm splits, muscle fibers unraveling like frayed rope. I feel the moment our livers separate, the capsule tearing, leaking bile that burns where it touches our shared peritoneum. Our lungs, which had grown together at the edges, rip apart with a sound like fabric, and suddenly I'm breathing only my own air, and it feels like drowning. I stumble back, and something rips between us. A cord. Thick, umbilical, veined with purple and blue, pulsing with shared blood, stretches from my abdomen to his. It snaps, wet and final, and the recoil sends us both sprawling. The end that was attached to me retracts into my navel, leaving a raw, weeping hole. The end attached to him whips around, spraying blood, still twitching with peristaltic movement, still trying to pump blood between us even though we're separate. I look up, and he has become a monster.

Not because he changed, but because I can see him from the outside now. His face is wrong. It's too familiar, too intimate, distorted by the separation. His jaw is too square, his brown eyes too knowing. His skin bears the wounds of our tearing. Raw patches formed where my skin tore away from his, revealing muscle underneath that looks like mine, not his. Pink, vulnerable, wrong. There are Indentations in his flesh where my ribs had pressed against his, bruises in the shape of my bones. His chest is open still, the ribcage splayed like broken fingers, and I can see his heart beating in there, but it's beating wrong. It was too fast, arrhythmic, trying to match a rhythm that isn't there anymore. He moves like he's learning to walk for the first time. His nervous system is trying to remember how to work without mine. He stumbles toward me, arms outstretched, making sounds that are half my name and half animal panic. He's incomplete. I took parts of him when I tore away, pieces of our shared intestine dangle from my midsection, and I realize with horror that I have his tissue inside me, necrotizing, dying because it doesn't belong. And he has pieces of me—my skin grafted to his chest in patches that are already turning gray, rejecting.

He chases me, limping and thrusting through the unfamiliar through the woods that have gone rotten. The trees slump like tired flesh. The ground is soft, yielding, like bruised skin. I run, but the paths loop back. I find myself at the spot where we tore apart, the impossibly black blood still wet, the cord still twitching on the ground, the shared organs, our liver, split in two like a rotten fruit, steaming in the cold air. He is behind me. I can hear his breathing- ragged, desperate, hopeless. I can hear the wet sound of his footsteps, the slap of his bare feet against the moss, and I realize he's leaving pieces of himself behind, a trail of tissue and fluid marking his pursuit. I turn.

He is grotesque, panting, bleeding from the separation wounds. His half-swept hair is matted with our shared blood, clumped and stiff. His scarred skin looks fresh, new, as if the act of tearing us apart created new injuries. Gashes where my fingernails had gripped him from the inside, trying to hold on even as I tried to escape. His chest is a cavity, ribs spread wide, and I can see his heart, but it's not just his heart anymore, it's ours, split down the middle, each ventricle pumping for a different body, the muscle tearing itself apart with every beat. He reaches for me with hands that are still partly fused. Fingers that end in raw, red tissue where they used to connect to mine, the skin hanging in flaps, the tendons exposed and glistening. His jaw works, trying to speak, but his vocal cords are still partially merged with mine, and the sound that comes out is a wet, choking gurgle, a harmony of two voices trying to use one throat. I should run. I should fight.

But I look at him, and I see that he is not a monster. He is not the man I loved, either. He is something in between the space where my boundary ended and his began, externalized and walking. He is the part of relationship that demands, that consumes, that refuses to let me be alone. He is my fear of the other made flesh, but he is also my desire for connection made manifest.  I stop running. I approach him slowly. He flinches, expecting violence, expecting rejection. I reach out slowly, cautiously, and touch his face, his jaw, that square, stubborn jaw, and I feel the scabs under my fingertips, the raised ridges of scar tissue, the heat of infection already setting in where our wounds meet the air. He is terrifying. He is needy. He is wounded. He is real.

I set a boundary. In my mind, I visualized a membrane, soap-thin, invisible but absolute. I let him touch me, but I remain separate. I let him love me, but I keep my thoughts in a room he cannot enter. The fusion was death. The isolation was death. This is the only way to live. He feels the change in me. His breathing slows, regaining its warmth and love. The monstrousness doesn't leave his face, but it softens into something else. Something beautiful. Something safe. Something acceptable. I open my eyes to see the same bright whites of the room I had entered one year prior.
