# Exposure

"You? There are no relations between us." The woman at the counter responded, "You must be mistaken." She looked more confused than anything, there was no way that an outsider, a person from above, had any relation to her. Still he did not leave- so she asked, "Why are you here?". "We're here for you, I... I'm here for you." There shone the slightest glimmer of hope in his eyes, a lighthouse that tried to bring her home to shore from the void that falls on the ocean at night. "And who are you people exactly?" "Besra, I'm Grant- your brother, this is my wife Manada. Anderson and Eira, and Christoph and Giesla." "Yes, you are due for testing, would you like to begin early?" She said looking at the empty terminal- she knew this was a lie, she remembered the emotions that led this man astray. There was some admiration for the depth he allowed his emotions to reach. She wanted to leave, she wanted to go back to her idle job doing nothing. She had never gone outside. In the 11 years at the front desk, only he had the audacity to come in and try to throw himself down the chute. In the 11 years at the front desk, she had never even thought of touching the sunlight that blinded her each day.

She thought that if she tried to leave the skies would descend on her and devour her whole, any movement that was un-regulated would do that. She had thought to jump as he did. She wanted to leave, go somewhere that was away from herself. "Have you ever thought about who your mother or father are?" The man named Grant questioned her with a determination that she had never seen below. Her hatred for him only intensified, "There is no reason for questions, please exit if you are not here for testing." "Besra," She hated the name already, "Please, at least come meet your father- he'll be happy to see you." "Please leave." Her smile descended from its high peaks, the full preparations she had made to rot at the front desk faded in front of her as he pushed her around. The noticeable change in her demeanor shook the man claiming to be his brother, "Are we getting you into trouble? Please get approval, I want to get to know you- we, we would like to get to know you." 

"No," she said before thinking of what he said- her discomfort rose like a bonfire doused in gasoline, "I haven't known you- I don't want to know you, please leave." She loved her tedium. She lived in her spot, there was no home for her outside. There was nothing for her. Maybe, if he had come in years past there had been a chance, there would be some decision she would make against her better judgement to leave. Maybe, if she had been better at what she did he would never have found her. But he was here now, he was trying to move her. He was trying to give her another chance. The resemblance of who she used to be- the girl that wanted to see things she couldn't see. The girl that had hopes and dreams for a future she had burned was dead, replaced by a thing. An empty thing that did not feel, or at least was not supposed to feel. Grant walked around the counter, crossing her barrier physically and emotionally when he gently wrapped his arms around her. She hoped that someone would let her leave, she wanted to go back to the world she knew- the world she was separate from. She hoped to an entity that was not there for him to leave her alone, but he held on. He always found a way to hold on.

Then Grant walked around the counter. He crossed the physical line, stepping into her sanctuary, and wrapped his arms around her. Her body locked instantly. She had not been touched in eleven years. It was unprofessional; it was forbidden; it was a violation of every rule that kept her alive. The smell of him, of the air from above, of sweat and dust and real sunlight, flooded her lungs, sickening her. She waited for him to let go, staring blankly over his shoulder at the empty hall, praying to whatever entity governed the facility for him to release her. He didn't. He held on with a terrifying, desperate strength.

Before her mind could process the intrusion, shadow fell across them. Anderson moved in next, stepping over the threshold without permission. Then came the others, drawn in by the momentum of Grant's conviction. One by one, bodies pressed against her. Fabric rubbed against fabric; hot breaths collided in the cramped space behind the desk. They enclosed her entirely, building a cage of physical heat. To them, it was an offering—a reunion, a restoration. To her, it was simply the overwhelming, suffocating weight of strangers' skin. She was trapped at the center of them, both more hidden and more exposed than she had ever been in her life. The rigid structure holding her together shattered.

A tear slipped free, hot and silent. Then another. The sound that escaped her chest was not a gentle sigh of relief, but a ragged, terrifying gasp as eleven years of carefully guarded silence caved in. The noise caught in the small space, spreading like a contagion until the group around her began to weep too; closing their arms tighter, believing they were sharing in her healing. But as the room filled with their tears of joy, hers remained the only tears of absolute grief. 

The storm inside her didn't resolve; it simply ran out of air. As the group's tight grip slowly loosened, Besra felt the familiar, cold numbness flood back into her chest. The circuit had tripped. The tears stopped as suddenly as they had begun, leaving her face wet and stiff in the air conditioned draft. She looked through the group and saw the hatch that led to the conveyer.

Grant pulled back slightly, his hands resting gently on her shoulders. His face was flushed, radiant with the misspent joy of a savior. "I am begging you, Besra," he whispered, searching her vacant eyes. "You don't have to stay here anymore. Please Besra, come with us." In the past, she had tried to choose. She had tried to decide what was best for her, wanting to leave a mark, and had been corrected. She had tried to reach for The Doctor in the breeding district, believing in a shared future, only to be left behind at this desk while he ascended. Resistance was a desire. Staying was a desire. She could not give in to her desires. To survive, she must be nothing.

"Okay," she said. The word was hollow, devoid of warmth or consent. It was simply the sound of a door being pushed open by a force larger than herself. Grant beamed, taking her hand. She didn't squeeze back, but she didn't pull away either. She looked down at the empty screen one last time. Eleven years of smooth glass, gray metal, and blinding morning light. She had no possessions to carry, nothing holding her in the position she resided in. Then, letting Grant pull her by the hand, She stepped out from behind the counter for the first time in four thousand days.

She stood directly behind the line where the shadow ended, terrified of the sunlight that threatened to liquefy her skin. The clear glass doors that were always open now laughed at her. Beyond the threshold, the world opened up into a sickening, infinite expanse. There was no ceiling. The morning sky above the parking lot was not blue, but a bruised, suffocating blood-red; a violent morning stain that stretched out forever, offering no walls, no corners, no boundaries. The vast open air didn't feel like space; it felt like being dropped into the middle of a bloody, bottomless ocean, where taking a single step meant suffocating in the sheer weight of empty air.

"Come on, Besra!" Grant called out, his voice loud and brimming with a terrifying, thoughtless excitement. He turned back and grabbed her wrist, his grip warm and solid, trying to yank her out onto the asphalt. He was moving too fast, desperate to drag her out to meet their father, completely blind to the fact that stepping across that threshold was flaying her alive.

"Grant, stop!" Manada’s voice cut through the cold air. She stepped forward, placing a firm, grounding hand on Grant's arm to pull him back. She looked at the rigid, frozen shape of the girl at the door with quiet, observant eyes. "Look at her. You're rushing her. Let her go at her own pace."

Grant blinked, his face falling slightly as he loosened his grip on her wrist, but he didn't let go entirely. "I just... I want her to see it. I want her to be free."

Free. The word made the cold void inside her coil with a sickening, silent hatred.

She stared at Grant's eager face, then past him at Manada, and then toward the idling vehicles where the rest of the group was disappearing. She didn't see people. She didn't see a brother, or a sister-in-law, or friends. She saw things. Loud, fleshy, moving things that operated with an arrogant, unearned warmth. They had found their places. They had found their matches, their purpose, their love—things she had buried two decades ago when she lay back on the cold laminate floor behind her desk.

She hated them. She hated them for dragging her out into this uncapped red hell. She hated them for demanding that she be a person when she had worked so hard to become nothing at all.

"Please," her voice came out as a sweet, high-register chime, completely detached from the absolute horror clawing at her mind. Her pleasant, front-desk smile stretched across her face, bright, symmetrical, and completely dead, while her eyes remained wide and vacant under the blood-red morning light. "Do not pull me. The floor outside has no walls."
